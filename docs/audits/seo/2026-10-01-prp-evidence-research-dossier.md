## Editorial and search decision

One informational article should answer the question “Wirkt PRP?” while explicitly separating **androgenetic alopecia** from **facial skin rejuvenation**. These are different indications, interventions and endpoints. The article must not be another generic “PRP Berlin” service page: `/aesthetik/prp-behandlung` owns local aesthetic-service intent and `/leistungen/prp-haarausfall` owns local hair-treatment intent. The article's conversion bridge is an individual consultation on the relevant existing service page, not a promise of benefit.

That is a discovery opportunity, not evidence that a new URL will rank or convert. ## Evidence matrix and claim-to-source map

Candidate wording below is **not approved public medical copy**. Each source is linked beside the narrow claim it supports; findings in one indication must not be transferred to another.

| Source and evidence type | Exact supported claim / cautious German wording candidate | Limitation | Intended paragraph |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Kieling et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39013743/), systematic review/meta-analysis of randomised trials in androgenetic alopecia | In 13 pooled trials, hair density favoured PRP over placebo; the authors rated the evidence **low quality** and identified high heterogeneity and publication bias. “Bei androgenetischem Haarausfall deuten Studien auf eine mögliche Zunahme der Haardichte hin; die Sicherheit der Aussage ist begrenzt.” | 431 participants in 14 studies for qualitative synthesis; disparate protocols and high heterogeneity (I² 95.99% for density). Hair diameter did not show a clear pooled benefit. No guarantee for an individual patient, other hair-loss causes or facial skin. | “Was ist für Haare untersucht?” first evidence paragraph |
| [Anitua et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40944844/), systematic review/meta-analysis of 43 RCTs across alopecia subtypes | A larger, newer synthesis also reports hair-density improvement in selected comparisons, while warning that protocol/composition heterogeneity and incomplete adverse-event reporting limit generalisation. “Auch neuere Übersichten finden Hinweise auf einen Nutzen, können aber keine verlässliche Wirkung für jede PRP-Zubereitung oder Ursache des Haarausfalls ableiten.” | Mixed alopecia types and comparators; androgenetic alopecia dominates; subgroup findings cannot establish equivalence across subtypes. Authors are affiliated with a regenerative-medicine/biotechnology institute; clinician should assess applicability to the clinic's actual protocol. | Hair evidence limitations, adjacent to the claim |
| [Qin et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40167104/), systematic review of 20 facial PRP/**PRF** studies (514 patients) | Results differed by outcome: improvement was reported more often for skin thickness/elasticity than for hydration, wrinkles, texture or dyschromia. “Für die Gesichtshaut ist die Studienlage je nach gemessenem Merkmal uneinheitlich.” | Mixes PRP and PRF; includes nonrandomised cohorts/case series and comparisons with baseline; the percentages of positive studies are **not** a patient's probability of success. Cannot support a universal anti-ageing claim. | “Was ist für Gesichtshaut untersucht?” opening paragraph |
| [Chen & Zhou, 2025/2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41694325/), meta-analysis of 9 RCTs (358 patients) in skin ageing | Pooled patient satisfaction and the authors' composite objective-efficacy measure favoured PRP, but the abstract explicitly says robust evidence is lacking. “Kleinere randomisierte Studien zeigen einzelne positive Ergebnisse; welche Veränderungen im Alltag zuverlässig eintreten, bleibt unsicher.” | Few small studies, varied outcomes and interventions; one composite RR does not establish improvement for every wrinkle, eye concern or treatment protocol. Review publication is dated Nov 2025, with 2026 collection year. | Face evidence counterweight, beside the preceding review |
| [Alam et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30419125/), masked split-face randomised trial of PRP vs saline for photoaged cheeks | Participants rated texture/wrinkles more improved on the PRP side at six months, but dermatologist-rated photoaging differences were not statistically significant. “Subjektive und verblindete ärztliche Bewertungen können auseinandergehen.” | Only 19 participants analysed; one injection; cheeks, not under-eye or scar outcomes. | Face evidence uncertainty paragraph |
| [Pincelli et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38798929/), small randomised split-face trial, PRP vs saline **with whole-face microneedling** | No notable PRP-versus-saline improvement in measured laxity, wrinkles or roughness; pain/burning occurred with both treatments. “Eine kleine kontrollierte Studie fand für die untersuchten Gesichtsmerkmale keinen Zusatznutzen gegenüber Kochsalzlösung.” | 18 women; both sides received microneedling, so this tests PRP's added value in that protocol, not all PRP monotherapy. | Same face evidence paragraph, explicit contrary finding |
| [Tsai et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38253883/), double-blind split-face trial, PRP vs platelet-poor plasma | Both sides improved on some scales without a significant overall between-group difference. “Eine kleine Studie konnte PRP nicht eindeutig von plättchenarmem Plasma abgrenzen.” | Ten completers; active plasma comparator, not no treatment; not generalisable to all facial concerns. | Face evidence limitations |
| [Alam et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30419125/) and [Pincelli et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38798929/), randomised facial trials | Temporary redness, swelling, bruising or discomfort were reported in these injection studies. “Auch bei Eigenblutverfahren sind vorübergehende lokale Beschwerden möglich.” | Small trials cannot establish the frequency of rare harms; the events cannot always be attributed specifically to PRP versus injection/microneedling. Do **not** say “risk-free”. | Risks/limitations paragraph |
| [Kramer & Keaney, 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29790267/), systematic review of PRP preparation in hair studies | Preparation and final composition were inconsistently reported, hindering comparison and protocol-specific conclusions. “PRP ist kein einheitlich hergestelltes Produkt; Ergebnisse verschiedener Verfahren lassen sich nicht eins zu eins übertragen.” | Older evidence and hair-focused, but directly supports the methodological point; no inference about Praxis Jona's preparation. | “Warum Studienergebnisse variieren” |

## Claims that must not appear

- “PRP wirkt nachweislich gegen Haarausfall” without restricting to the studied diagnosis and uncertainty; no guaranteed regrowth or fixed number of sessions.
- “PRP verjüngt die Haut”, “beseitigt Augenringe/Falten/Narben” or “wirkt bei allen Hautproblemen”. These outcomes were not established across protocols and indications.
- “Eigenblut = nebenwirkungsfrei” or an absence of rare harms inferred from small trials.
- Treat PRF, microneedling-plus-PRP, scalp PRP and facial PRP as interchangeable.
- Use study-level percentages or pooled relative risks as a patient's personal chance of success.
- Claim equivalence or superiority to minoxidil, hair transplantation, fillers or other options from placebo-focused reviews.
- Present platelet growth factors or a plausible mechanism as proof of clinical effectiveness.

## Proposed article structure and link architecture

1. Direct answer: evidence depends on the **indication** and outcome; neither hair nor face results are guaranteed.
2. What PRP is and why preparation differences matter. Practice-specific procedure details remain blank until confirmed.
3. Hair: restrict evidence discussion to androgenetic alopecia; explain density versus diameter, study quality, diagnostic assessment and alternatives without treatment advice.
4. Face: distinguish texture, elasticity, wrinkles, pigmentation and hydration; pair positive syntheses with neutral/negative randomised trials.
5. What we do **not** know: patient selection, durability, optimal protocol, under-eye claims and rare-harm frequency.
6. Individual consultation: diagnosis, suitability, alternatives, costs and informed expectations. Link to the appropriate service page, not directly from every paragraph to booking.
7. Source list and author/reviewer attribution with the actual clinician review date when supplied.

Planned incoming links after approval: contextual evidence/limitations sections of `/aesthetik/prp-behandlung`, `/leistungen/prp-haarausfall`, and one relevant face-child page such as `/aesthetik/prp-behandlung/prp-gesicht`. Outgoing links: those two service owners and `/blog/authors/jonida-gjolli` **only if** Dr. Gjolli confirms authorship/review. Do not introduce an alternate canonical local PRP landing page.
