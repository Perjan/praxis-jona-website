## Editorial Decision

The article should not promise that an iron infusion “works” after a fixed number of days. It should answer the search intent by separating three different outcomes:

1. **Iron availability and stores:** intravenous iron delivers iron directly, but faster replenishment does not guarantee that a patient feels better immediately.
2. **Haematological response:** in iron-deficiency anaemia, haemoglobin response is commonly assessed after approximately two to four weeks; the individual course depends on the diagnosis, ongoing losses, inflammation and other conditions.
3. **Symptoms such as fatigue:** selected trials report improvement over roughly four to eight weeks, but results vary by population and at least one large placebo-controlled donor trial found no fatigue benefit at six to eight weeks.

The defensible answer is therefore: **laboratory and symptom responses have different timelines; improvement is individual and not guaranteed.** Diagnosis, indication, product, dose, ongoing iron loss and the cause of symptoms all matter.

## Proposed Answer-First Passage

> **Wann eine Eiseninfusion spürbar wirkt, lässt sich nicht auf einen festen Tag festlegen.** Die Eisenversorgung verändert sich nach der Infusion, doch Laborwerte und Beschwerden folgen nicht demselben Zeitplan. Bei einer Eisenmangelanämie wird die Hämoglobin-Reaktion häufig nach etwa zwei bis vier Wochen kontrolliert. Ob und wann sich Müdigkeit oder Leistungsfähigkeit bessern, ist individueller; Studien untersuchten solche Veränderungen meist über mehrere Wochen und zeigen keine garantierte Wirkung für jede Person. Entscheidend sind der bestätigte Eisenmangel, die Ursache, fortbestehende Blutverluste oder Entzündungen, Präparat und Dosis sowie andere mögliche Ursachen der Beschwerden.

This passage is a **draft for clinician approval**, not publication-ready medical copy.

## Evidence Matrix

| Source | Evidence type | What it supports | Important limitation / editorial use |

| [Snook et al., British Society of Gastroenterology guideline, 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8515119/) | Clinical guideline | Parenteral iron replenishes stores more quickly than oral iron, but haemoglobin rise is often similar or only marginally faster. A satisfactory Hb response after IV iron should be monitored after about 2–4 weeks. IV iron is considered when oral iron is contraindicated, ineffective, not tolerated, unlikely to work or urgent correction is needed. | Primarily adult iron-deficiency anaemia and UK practice. Use for the lab-monitoring window and indication nuance, not a universal symptom promise. |
| [DeLoughery et al., AGA Clinical Practice Update, 2024](https://gastro.org/clinical-guidance/management-of-iron-deficiency-anemia/) | Expert review / best-practice advice | IV iron is appropriate when oral iron is not tolerated, ferritin fails to improve after an oral trial or absorption is unlikely. Formulations that can replace the deficit in one or two infusions are preferred. True anaphylaxis is very rare; many reactions are infusion reactions rather than IgE-mediated allergy. | Gastroenterology-focused expert review. Do not convert indication advice into a claim that IV iron is better for everyone. |
| [Dugan et al., abridged Cochrane review and meta-analysis, 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36321348/) | Systematic review and meta-analysis; 21 RCTs, 3,514 adults with non-anaemic iron deficiency | IV iron improved ferritin and haemoglobin and produced a small pooled reduction in fatigue versus placebo; no overall quality-of-life difference was found. Mild adverse events were more frequent. | Authors remained uncertain because evidence quality was low or very low for most outcomes and included populations were heterogeneous. Use to frame possible benefit with uncertainty, never guaranteed symptom relief. |
| [Krayenbuehl et al., 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21705493/) | Double-blind RCT; 90 fatigued, non-anaemic premenopausal women | Outcomes were assessed at 6 and 12 weeks. The overall six-week fatigue difference was not statistically significant; a benefit appeared in the subgroup with ferritin ≤15 ng/mL. | Small, narrow population and subgroup-dependent result. It does not establish a universal ferritin threshold or response timeline. |
| [Favrat et al., PREFER trial, 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24751822/) | Randomised placebo-controlled trial; 290 premenopausal women with symptomatic iron deficiency and normal/borderline Hb | More participants receiving ferric carboxymaltose met the fatigue-response definition by day 56 than placebo. | Narrow population and product-specific intervention. | [Keller et al., blood donor trial, 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32848185/) | Randomised placebo-controlled trial; 405 non-anaemic iron-deficient blood donors | IV ferric carboxymaltose corrected iron markers but did not improve fatigue or general well-being at 6–8 weeks versus placebo. | Healthy repeat-donor population. Valuable counterevidence showing biochemical correction and felt benefit are not interchangeable. |
| [AWMF S3 guideline: preoperative anaemia, version 2.0, 2026](https://www.awmf.org/aktuelles/awmf-aktuell/diagnostik-und-therapie-der-praeoperativen-anaemie) | Current German S3 guideline | Reinforces rational diagnosis and treatment of anaemia and the need for interdisciplinary implementation. | Perioperative population; use for the principle of diagnosis before treatment, not outpatient symptom timing. |

Do not imply equal risk across all IV iron preparations; the article must name the preparation context if this claim is used. |

## Claim Candidates For Clinician Approval

Each item requires an explicit **approve / revise / reject** decision before drafting the public article.

- [ ] **No fixed day:** “Es gibt keinen festen Tag, an dem eine Eiseninfusion bei jeder Person spürbar wirkt.”
- [ ] **Two timelines:** “Laborwerte können sich verändern, bevor oder ohne dass Beschwerden im gleichen Maß nachlassen.”
- [ ] **Hb monitoring:** “Bei Eisenmangelanämie wird die Hämoglobin-Reaktion nach intravenösem Eisen häufig nach etwa zwei bis vier Wochen kontrolliert.”
- [ ] **Symptoms:** “Studien zu Müdigkeit untersuchten Veränderungen meist über mehrere Wochen; die Ergebnisse sind je nach Patientengruppe unterschiedlich.”
- [ ] **No guarantee:** “Eine Infusion garantiert keine Besserung von Müdigkeit, Konzentration, Leistungsfähigkeit oder Haarausfall.”
- [ ] **Individual factors:** “Ursache und Ausmaß des Mangels, Anämie, fortbestehende Blutverluste, Entzündungen, Präparat, Dosis und andere Erkrankungen beeinflussen den Verlauf.”
- [ ] **Follow-up:** “Ausbleibende oder zunehmende Beschwerden sollten ärztlich abgeklärt werden, statt automatisch eine weitere Infusion anzunehmen.”
- [ ] **Product-specific safety:** “Bei ferric-carboxymaltosehaltigen Präparaten kann eine Hypophosphatämie auftreten; zunehmende Müdigkeit zusammen mit Muskel- oder Knochenschmerzen sollte ärztlich beurteilt werden.”

## What We Are Not Claiming

- No immediate or guaranteed energy boost.
- No universal “you will feel better after X days” promise.
- No universal ferritin threshold, dose or number of infusions.
- No claim that IV iron is superior to oral iron for every patient.
- No inference that fatigue, hair loss, poor concentration or reduced performance is necessarily caused by iron deficiency.
- No claim that normalising ferritin necessarily produces subjective improvement.
- No claim that all IV iron preparations share the same adverse-effect profile.
- No suggestion that a reader should self-diagnose or seek infusion without a clinical indication.

## Proposed Article Structure

1. **Direct answer:** no fixed day; distinguish laboratory response from symptom response.
2. **What “Wirkung” can mean:** iron availability, Hb, fatigue, exercise capacity, hair changes.
3. **A realistic timeline:** what may be assessed in the first days, at 2–4 weeks and later, with no promise at any stage.
4. **Why the timeline varies:** confirmed deficiency, anaemia, cause, ongoing losses, inflammation, formulation/dose and alternative diagnoses.
5. **What research says about fatigue:** balanced summary of the meta-analysis, selected positive trials and negative donor trial.
6. **When follow-up matters:** planned lab/clinical review and what non-response may mean.
7. **Risks and red flags:** infusion reactions, product-specific hypophosphataemia context and when to seek medical advice.
8. **Who may be considered for IV iron:** only after diagnosis and an individual indication; oral intolerance/ineffectiveness, likely malabsorption or urgency as examples.
9. **Praxis Jona process:** consultation, laboratory review, individual decision, infusion and follow-up.
10. **FAQ:** concise answers that mirror visible copy exactly if FAQ schema is used.
