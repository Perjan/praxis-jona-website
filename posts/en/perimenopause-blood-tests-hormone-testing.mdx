You want to know whether your symptoms are hormone-related. A blood test seems the obvious answer. In perimenopause, however, one hormone result is often less clear than hoped.

## Why do levels fluctuate?

Hormone production changes within a cycle and, during the transition, between cycles too. A blood sample is a snapshot. A normal result does not reliably exclude perimenopause. Conversely, an abnormal result alone does not provide a comprehensive diagnosis.

## From 45, clinical assessment usually comes first

With typical symptoms, age, new cycle changes and medical history are assessed together. A routine large hormone profile is usually unnecessary. This does not make symptoms less real: treatment discussions need not wait for a laboratory result to provide supposedly perfect proof.

## When can hormone tests help?

Targeted investigation is particularly important when an early transition before 45 or premature reduction in ovarian function before 40 is suspected. Which values and repeat measurements are needed depends on the specific question. Medicines and contraception must also be considered.

## Progesterone at the start of the cycle: what does it show?

Low progesterone is physiological at this stage. To establish whether ovulation occurred, measurement must be timed to the suspected ovulation. A blanket “deficiency test” on days 1 to 5 does not answer that question.

## Which other blood tests may be useful?

With fatigue or reduced performance, medical history may justify looking for anaemia, iron deficiency or thyroid dysfunction, for example. Heavy bleeding, diet and further symptoms influence this decision. This does not establish a fixed laboratory package for every woman.

## What should you not decide yourself from results?

Do not start or change hormone treatment based on an isolated laboratory finding. A broad reference range also does not allow a personal optimal value to be set arbitrarily. What matters is whether the result usefully supports a specific treatment decision.

## Preparing for the appointment

Bring existing results with their dates and, if known, cycle days. Record hormonal medicines, contraception and bleeding patterns. Ask: which question should the test answer? Would its result change treatment? This keeps investigations understandable and targeted.
