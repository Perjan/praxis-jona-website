“Is it too much oestrogen or too little progesterone?” This is understandable when bleeding, sleep and mood change. However, hormonal processes are more complex than many symptom charts suggest.

## What happens to progesterone?

After ovulation, the corpus luteum produces progesterone. Without ovulation, the typical rise in the second half of the cycle is absent. Such cycles become more frequent during the menopausal transition. This is an important part of physiology, but not an explanation for every individual symptom.

Progesterone is not constant even within a normal cycle. A low value at the beginning is expected. It does not alone prove a deficiency requiring treatment.

## Is oestrogen always low in perimenopause?

No. Oestradiol can fluctuate strongly and sometimes be higher. The ovaries do not stop working progressively and evenly from a fixed day. Only after the transition does ovarian oestrogen production remain significantly lower.

## What does “oestrogen dominance” mean?

The term often describes a relative predominance of oestrogen effects over progesterone. However, it is not a standardised diagnosis validated through a symptom list. Different providers mean different things by it. A quiz result saying “you have oestrogen dominance” would therefore imply more certainty than the answers support.

## Which symptoms can actually be attributed?

Absent ovulation can affect bleeding patterns. Breast tenderness or cycle-related symptoms may occur at the same time. Sleep problems, anxiety, fatigue and brain fog are particularly non-specific. Several symptoms occur in different hormonal situations. Medicines, psychological stress and other conditions may also contribute.

A personal hormone ratio cannot be calculated or reliably estimated from these symptoms. An individual laboratory result must also fit the question, cycle and treatment.

## Is there “progesterone dominance”?

It is not a separate recognised stage of menopause. During treatment, fatigue or dizziness may occur, for example. The assessment then considers a connection with the preparation, timing or another cause. This does not create another natural menopausal stage.

## What is more useful than a hormone label?

Describe changes as specifically as possible: is bleeding heavier? Do night sweats wake you? Are symptoms continuous or limited to certain cycle days? This information helps with decisions about investigations and treatment far more than a blanket self-diagnosis.
