An iron infusion can be an important treatment. However, it should follow a justified treatment plan, rather than fatigue or a single ferritin result alone.

## What reasons may support an infusion?

Oral iron is not always sufficiently tolerated or absorbed. A lack of response despite an appropriate treatment trial, or certain underlying conditions, can also make the intravenous route useful. Initial findings, anaemia, symptoms and the cause of deficiency determine the decision.

## What is checked beforehand?

Assessment includes the relevant laboratory values and the cause of deficiency. Iron parameters are interpreted differently with inflammation. The previous preparation, elemental dose, treatment duration and intake conditions should also be known. Without this context, “tablets do not work” would be too imprecise.

## The cause remains important

An infusion does not resolve an ongoing source of bleeding. An absorption disorder or inflammatory condition also deserves its own treatment. The plan should therefore include monitoring and further steps, rather than ending with the infusion.

## Which risks are discussed?

Infusion reactions, among other problems, can occur during or after administration. Medical monitoring is therefore part of treatment. Which risks are particularly important also depends on the preparation used and your medical history.

## Why phosphate can matter

Hypophosphataemia, meaning low blood phosphate, is a known risk with ferric carboxymaltose. The FDA highlighted this again in 2026. Fatigue, muscle weakness or bone pain after administration should not automatically be treated as continuing iron deficiency.

Whether and when phosphate is checked depends on the preparation, risk factors and repeated treatment, among other considerations. This warning specifically concerns the relevant preparation; it does not mean all iron infusions carry the same risk. A laboratory decrease is also not the same as severe symptomatic illness.

## How is the response monitored?

Immediately after an infusion, ferritin cannot be interpreted like an untreated baseline result. The appropriate monitoring time is therefore planned. A temporarily high value does not prove permanent iron overload and is not a reason for further decisions on your own.

If you take tablets and progress remains unclear, the [iron tablets check](/en/free-tools/iron-tablets-check) can help prepare your consultation. It does not decide whether you need an infusion.
